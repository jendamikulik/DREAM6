\title{\textbf{Generated Complexity, Certificate Complexity,\\
and Discovery Complexity}\\[0.4em]
\large Closing the Effective-Face Rank-Collapse Diagnostic}
\author{}
\date{August 28, 2026}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
A previous numerical audit of the quartic DLE Bellman solver found a
striking discrepancy between the entropy-weighted effective active-face
count and the linear rank of the corresponding near-active cut normals.
At the certified roots, the former grows into the thousands, whereas the
latter rapidly saturates the ambient affine dimension.

We close the first layer of this observation analytically.

First, the soft effective-face count is not an invariant of the
underlying polyhedral function: duplicating an active affine piece leaves
the function, its epigraph, its zero set, and its subdifferential
unchanged, while making the effective-face count arbitrarily large.

Second, for every fixed finite-dimensional root-master LP, an optimality
certificate exists whose active inequality-normal support has cardinality
at most the affine dimension of the simplex.  For the present support
cutoff \(M=8\), this gives a certificate involving at most eight active
inequality normals, including Bellman cuts and active simplex-boundary
constraints.

The resulting distinction is therefore not merely between ``many cuts''
and ``few directions.''  Three different complexity notions must be
separated:
\[
\text{generated complexity},\qquad
\text{certificate complexity},\qquad
\text{certificate-discovery complexity}.
\]
The first can be very large while the second is dimensionally bounded.
The remaining question is how difficult it is for an exact Bellman
separation procedure to discover the sparse certificate.
\end{abstract}


% ============================================================
\section{Polyhedral Bellman setting}
% ============================================================

Fix the finite probability simplex
\[
\Delta_M
=
\left\{
a\in\R^{M+1}:
a_j\ge0,\quad
\mathbf 1^\top a=1
\right\}.
\]

At Bellman depth \(r\), let the polyhedral defect be represented as
\[
d_r(a)
=
\max_{\alpha\in\Acal_r}
\left\{
g_{r,\alpha}^{\top}a-h_{r,\alpha}
\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]

Write
\[
c_\alpha(a)
=
g_{r,\alpha}^{\top}a-h_{r,\alpha},
\qquad
\widehat d_r(a)
=
\max_\alpha c_\alpha(a),
\]
and define the facet gap
\[
\Delta_\alpha(a)
=
\widehat d_r(a)-c_\alpha(a)
\ge0.
\tag{2}
\]

For a scale \(\tau>0\), the previously proposed soft effective-face
diagnostic is
\[
w_\alpha(a)
=
\exp\left(
-\frac{\Delta_\alpha(a)}{\tau}
\right),
\]
\[
p_\alpha(a)
=
\frac{w_\alpha(a)}
{\sum_\beta w_\beta(a)},
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
N_{\mathrm{eff}}(a)
=
\frac{1}{\sum_\alpha p_\alpha(a)^2}.
}
\tag{3}
\]

The intended interpretation was that
\[
N_{\mathrm{eff}}\ll|\Acal_r|
\]
indicates a locally head-dominated polyhedral geometry.


% ============================================================
\section{\texorpdfstring{$N_{\mathrm{eff}}$}{Neff} is not a geometric invariant}
% ============================================================

The numerical rank audit suggested that \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}\) responds
strongly to multiplicity of near-active cuts.  This can be proved
directly.

\begin{theorem}[Duplication instability of \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}\)]
Let
\[
f(a)
=
\max_{\alpha\in\Acal}
\{\ell_\alpha(a)\}
\]
be a polyhedral convex function, where
\[
\ell_\alpha(a)
=
g_\alpha^\top a-h_\alpha.
\]

Fix a point \(a_\star\) and suppose that one affine piece
\(\ell_\star\) is active there:
\[
\ell_\star(a_\star)=f(a_\star).
\]

For \(K\ge1\), construct a new representation by adjoining \(K\)
additional exact copies of \(\ell_\star\).

Then:

\[
f_K=f,
\]
\[
\epi f_K=\epi f,
\]
\[
\{a:f_K(a)=0\}
=
\{a:f(a)=0\},
\]
and
\[
\partial f_K(a)=\partial f(a)
\qquad
\text{for every }a.
\]

Nevertheless,
\[
N_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(K)}(a_\star)
\longrightarrow\infty
\qquad
\text{as }K\to\infty.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Adding exact copies of an affine function already present in a maximum
does not alter the maximum itself.  Hence all geometric objects
determined by the function remain unchanged.

At \(a_\star\), every duplicate of the active piece has zero facet gap,
hence weight
\[
w=1.
\]

Let the total weight and squared-weight sum of all original,
nonduplicated pieces be
\[
W=\sum_\alpha w_\alpha,
\qquad
S=\sum_\alpha w_\alpha^2.
\]

After adjoining \(K\) copies,
\[
N_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(K)}
=
\frac{(W+K)^2}{S+K}.
\]

Therefore
\[
N_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(K)}
\sim K
\qquad
(K\to\infty).
\]

Thus \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}\) can be made arbitrarily large without changing
the represented polyhedral function.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
This establishes a stronger statement than the empirical rank
observation alone:
\[
\boxed{
N_{\mathrm{eff}}
\text{ is representation-dependent.}
}
\]

It cannot by itself be interpreted as an intrinsic measure of
polyhedral complexity.
\end{remark}


% ============================================================
\section{What the rank-collapse experiment actually established}
% ============================================================

For the quartic DLE experiment with support cutoff
\[
M=8,
\]
the certified-root measurements included

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
r & |\Acal_r| & N_{\mathrm{eff}}(a_r^\star)
\\
\hline
2 & 1295 & 1115.9
\\
3 & 2611 & 2073.5
\end{array}
\tag{4}
\]

for \(\tau=10^{-2}\).

At the same points, define
\[
S_\varepsilon(a_r^\star)
=
\left\{
\alpha:
\Delta_\alpha(a_r^\star)<\varepsilon
\right\}.
\]

The observed rank table was

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
r & \varepsilon & |S_\varepsilon| &
\rank G(S_\varepsilon)
\\
\hline
2 & 10^{-9} & 1    & 1\\
2 & 10^{-4} & 51   & 8\\
2 & 10^{-3} & 206  & 8\\
2 & 10^{-2} & 1063 & 8\\
\midrule
3 & 10^{-9} & 7    & 5\\
3 & 10^{-4} & 98   & 8\\
3 & 10^{-3} & 351  & 8\\
3 & 10^{-2} & 1779 & 8
\end{array}
\tag{5}
\]

where the rank is understood modulo the all-ones gauge.

Since
\[
\Delta_8
\subset
\{a\in\R^9:\mathbf 1^\top a=1\}
\]
has affine dimension \(8\), the terminal rank \(8\) is the ambient
ceiling.

Thus the empirical statement should be formulated as

\[
\boxed{
\text{near-active multiplicity grows enormously after the normal space
has already saturated.}
}
\tag{6}
\]

This does \emph{not} imply that all subsequent inequalities are
polyhedrally redundant.

It does imply that the soft effective-face count and directional rank
measure fundamentally different objects.


% ============================================================
\section{Sparse optimality certificates}
% ============================================================

The root master at level \(r\) has the form
\[
\begin{aligned}
\min_{a\in\R^{M+1}}\quad&
c^\top a
\\
\text{s.t.}\quad&
g_i^\top a\le h_i,
\qquad i\in I,
\\
&
a_j\ge0,
\qquad j=0,\ldots,M,
\\
&
\mathbf 1^\top a=1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

Its feasible set lies in an affine space of dimension \(M\).

The relevant fact is not that the whole feasible polytope has a small
facet description.  In general it need not.

The relevant fact is that a \emph{single optimum} admits a sparse dual
certificate.

\begin{theorem}[Dimension-bounded optimality certificate]
Let \(a^\star\) solve the root-master LP \((7)\).

Then there exists an optimality certificate at \(a^\star\) involving
at most
\[
\boxed{M}
\]
active inequality normals in total, where these normals may come from

\[
g_i^\top a\le h_i
\]
or from active simplex-boundary constraints
\[
-a_j\le0.
\]

The equality
\[
\mathbf 1^\top a=1
\]
is not counted among these \(M\) inequalities.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let
\[
T
=
\{v\in\R^{M+1}:\mathbf 1^\top v=0\}
\]
be the tangent space of the affine hull of the simplex.  Then
\[
\dim T=M.
\]

The KKT conditions imply that the negative projected objective direction
belongs to the cone generated by the projected normals of active
inequality constraints:
\[
-\Pi_T c
\in
\cone
\left\{
\Pi_T n_i:
i\text{ active at }a^\star
\right\},
\tag{8}
\]
where the \(n_i\) include both Bellman-cut normals and active simplex
boundary normals.

By the conic Carath\'eodory theorem in the \(M\)-dimensional vector
space \(T\), every vector in this cone can be represented as a
nonnegative combination of at most \(M\) generators.

Hence there exists another KKT representation of the same projected
objective direction using at most \(M\) active inequality normals.
Together with the simplex equality, these normals certify optimality of
\(a^\star\).
\end{proof}

For the present computation,
\[
M=8.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\text{every fixed root optimum admits an optimality certificate
using at most eight active inequality normals.}
}
\tag{9}
\]

This bound concerns \emph{certificate size}, not the number of facets
needed to describe the entire root viability polytope.


% ============================================================
\section{Three distinct complexity notions}
% ============================================================

The previous observations suggest separating three quantities.

\paragraph{Generated complexity.}
Let
\[
\boxed{
C_{\mathrm{gen}}(r)
=
|\Acal_r|
}
\tag{10}
\]
denote the number of cuts generated and stored by the Bellman solver.

\paragraph{Certificate complexity.}
For a root optimum \(a_r^\star\), let
\[
\boxed{
C_{\mathrm{cert}}(r)
}
\tag{11}
\]
denote the minimum number of active inequality normals required in an
optimality certificate.

The preceding theorem gives
\[
\boxed{
C_{\mathrm{cert}}(r)\le M.
}
\tag{12}
\]

Thus at fixed support cutoff,
\[
C_{\mathrm{cert}}(r)
\]
is dimensionally bounded even if
\[
C_{\mathrm{gen}}(r)
\]
grows without apparent bound.

\paragraph{Discovery complexity.}
Let
\[
\boxed{
C_{\mathrm{disc}}(r)
}
\tag{13}
\]
denote the amount of exact separation work required before an algorithm
has generated enough information to expose an optimality certificate.

Unlike \(C_{\mathrm{cert}}\), this quantity is not controlled by
Carath\'eodory's theorem.

The observed computational phenomenon can therefore be summarized as

\[
\boxed{
\text{large generated model}
\quad\longrightarrow\quad
\text{small final certificate}
\quad\text{but possibly expensive discovery}.
}
\tag{14}
\]


% ============================================================
\section{Why rank alone cannot prune the pool}
% ============================================================

A necessary distinction is worth making explicit.

Suppose a family of inequalities in a two-dimensional affine space has
normal rank two.  It may nevertheless describe a polygon with
arbitrarily many essential facets.

Hence
\[
\boxed{
\text{linear dependence of normals}
\not\Longrightarrow
\text{polyhedral redundancy of inequalities}.
}
\tag{15}
\]

The rank-collapse observation therefore does not justify deleting every
cut whose normal lies in the span of previously retained normals.

The correct computational object is instead an optimality certificate:
one asks which cuts are required to certify the particular solution
currently relevant to the Bellman recursion.

This is a weaker requirement than globally reproducing the entire
polyhedral function, but it is exactly the requirement faced by the
root-master optimization.


% ============================================================
\section{Dual-support audit}
% ============================================================

The preceding theorem gives an existence result.  The natural empirical
test is to inspect the actual HiGHS dual solution.

For the full root master, let
\[
\lambda_i\ge0
\]
be the multiplier of Bellman cut \(i\).

Define
\[
I_{\mathrm{dual}}
=
\{i:\lambda_i>\varepsilon_{\mathrm{dual}}\}.
\]

Then solve the root master again using only the cuts in
\(I_{\mathrm{dual}}\), together with the simplex constraints.

If
\[
v(I_{\mathrm{dual}})
=
v(I)
\]
to numerical tolerance, the selected cuts constitute an explicit sparse
certificate for the current finite cut pool.

The crucial empirical ratios are therefore

\[
\boxed{
|\Acal_r|
\quad\longrightarrow\quad
|I_{\mathrm{dual}}|
}
\tag{16}
\]

and, after optional exact or greedy certificate reduction,

\[
\boxed{
|\Acal_r|
\quad\longrightarrow\quad
C_{\mathrm{cert}}^{\mathrm{observed}}(r).
}
\tag{17}
\]

If, for example,
\[
2611
\quad\longrightarrow\quad
5,
\]
then the computational bottleneck is clearly not the dimensional size
of the final certificate.

It is the process by which that certificate is discovered.


% ============================================================
\section{The Bellman-dual version}
% ============================================================

The root master is only the outermost optimization.

A stronger test concerns the Fenchel-dual Bellman step itself.

Suppose
\[
d_r(b)
=
\max_{i\in\Acal_r}
\{g_i^\top b-h_i\}.
\]

In the dual Bellman formulation, the conjugate contribution associated
with child \(x\) is represented using convex multipliers
\[
\alpha_i^{(x)}\ge0,
\qquad
\sum_i\alpha_i^{(x)}=1.
\]

Define the dual support
\[
S_x
=
\left\{
i:
\alpha_i^{(x)}>0
\right\}.
\]

The relevant statistic is

\[
\boxed{
s_r
=
\max_x |S_x|.
}
\tag{18}
\]

If
\[
s_r\ll|\Acal_r|
\]
systematically along the recursion, then sparse-certificate behavior is
not merely an outer-master artifact.

It is present inside the Bellman operator itself.

This would indicate a recursively sparse dual geometry despite a rapidly
growing explicit cut representation.


% ============================================================
\section{Reformulated open problem}
% ============================================================

The original basis-count question should therefore be replaced by a
discovery question.

\begin{problem}[Certificate-discovery complexity]
Fix \(M\) and the quartic Bellman recursion.

Although every finite-dimensional root master admits an optimality
certificate supported on at most \(M\) active inequality normals, how
many distinct Bellman cuts must an exact lazy-separation algorithm
generate before such a certificate is exposed?

Determine the growth of
\[
C_{\mathrm{disc}}(r)
\]
with Bellman depth \(r\).

Is it
\[
O(1),
\qquad
O(\log r),
\qquad
r^\alpha,
\]
or comparable to the total generated cut count
\[
|\Acal_r|?
\]
\end{problem}

A recursive version asks the same question for the support of the
Fenchel-dual multipliers at every Bellman level.


% ============================================================
\section{Possible implication for the DLE computation}
% ============================================================

The distinction
\[
C_{\mathrm{gen}}
\neq
C_{\mathrm{cert}}
\neq
C_{\mathrm{disc}}
\]
suggests a new direction for the head--tail solver.

The current implementation primarily compresses the \emph{stored}
constraint set.

A more aggressive architecture would instead attempt to preserve a
small \emph{certificate model}, for example through

\begin{itemize}
\item dual-support retention,
\item aggregate supporting planes,
\item bundle-style memory,
\item certificate-aware cut promotion,
\item or a small active basis followed by exact separation against the
complete archive.
\end{itemize}

The archive can remain exact.

Only the model explicitly presented to the LP need be small.

This preserves the logical contract

\[
\boxed{
\text{no omitted cut is assumed satisfied;
all omitted cuts remain available to the separation oracle.}
}
\tag{19}
\]


% ============================================================
\section{Conclusion}
% ============================================================

The effective-face rank-collapse experiment has two different layers.

The first can now be closed.

\[
\boxed{
N_{\mathrm{eff}}
\text{ is not an intrinsic geometric complexity measure.}
}
\]

It can be made arbitrarily large by representational duplication while
the underlying polyhedral function remains exactly unchanged.

The second layer is more informative.

For a fixed support cutoff \(M\), every root-master optimum has a sparse
optimality certificate of size at most \(M\), even though the Bellman
solver may generate thousands of cuts before finding it.

Thus the central computational question is no longer

\[
\text{``How many cuts exist?''}
\]

but

\[
\boxed{
\text{``How much work is required to discover the few cuts that
certify the relevant optimum?''}
}
\]

For the quartic DLE problem, this separates

\[
\boxed{
\text{generated complexity},
\qquad
\text{certificate complexity},
\qquad
\text{discovery complexity}.
}
\]

The first is empirically large.

The second is dimensionally bounded.

The third is the remaining open problem.
