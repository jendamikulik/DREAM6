\section*{Post-Intervention Checkpoint:
Replicable Safe Anchors and Phase-Coarse Renormalization}

\subsection*{1. Current theorem status}

For the explicit quartic pair
\[
P=\left(\frac18,\frac14,\frac14,\frac14,\frac18\right),
\qquad
Q=\left(\frac7{64},\frac5{16},\frac5{32},\frac5{16},\frac7{64}\right),
\]
with
\[
G_P(z)-G_Q(z)=\frac{(1-z)^4}{64},
\]
the rigorous lower bound remains
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal B_n=\Omega(\log n).
}
\]

A matching upper bound would follow from a scale-independent doubling estimate
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal B_{2n}\le \mathcal B_n+C.
}
\]

The missing step is still a prefix-compatible continuation theorem.
The numerical work below does not prove this theorem, but substantially
sharpens the form that the missing lemma should take.

\[

With
\[

Hence the normalized quartic defect is marginal:

\frac{\chi'}{\chi}
=

At the same point the fourth centered moment is fixed, whereas the

M_4'=M_4,

\boxed{

\subsection*{3. Reduced safe geometry}

In scaled coordinates
\[
y=
\left(
m,\frac{\mu_2}{2},\frac{\mu_4}{10}
\right),
\qquad
T_s=
\left(
\frac79,\frac23,\frac35
\right),
\]
let
\[
\mathcal R_m
=
F_m\mathcal P_m
\]
be the reduced reachable region.

Its Farkas margin is
\[
\delta_m(y)
=
\inf_{\|z\|_1=1}
\left[
h_m(z)-z\cdot y
\right],
\]
where
\[
h_m(z)
=
\sup_{\pi\in\mathcal P_m}
z\cdot F_m\pi.
\]

The condition
\[
\delta_m(y)\ge \rho
\]
is equivalent to the reduced inball condition
\[
y+\rho[-1,1]^3
\subseteq
\mathcal R_m.
\]

The reduced repair lemma therefore remains
\[
\|\pi'-\pi\|_1
\le
\frac{2\epsilon}{\rho+\epsilon}
\le
\frac{2}{\rho}\epsilon,
\]
provided
\[
\epsilon=\|F_m\pi-y\|_\infty
\]
and
\[
y+\rho[-1,1]^3
\subseteq\mathcal R_m.
\]

This is a reduced-profile statement only; it is not yet a full
history-level Hoffman estimate.

\subsection*{4. Certified compressed Farkas oracle}

The iterated renormalization produces extremely small positive tail atoms.
Typical observed scales include
\[
10^{-15},
\quad
10^{-30},
\quad
10^{-66},
\quad
10^{-110}.
\]

These tails are structurally generated by the renormalization and are not
specific to phase-extreme controller branches.

A certified compressed Kantorovich oracle was therefore introduced.
The original feature matrix is retained, while tiny-tail indices are
clustered only inside the dual representation.

For every compressed block \(G\times H\) and profile coordinate \(k\),
define
\[
L_{GH,k}
=
\min_{(i,j)\in G\times H}F_{k,ij},
\qquad
U_{GH,k}
=
\max_{(i,j)\in G\times H}F_{k,ij}.
\]

This produces rigorous lower and upper compressed support functions and
hence a bracket
\[
\boxed{
\delta_{\rm lower}(y)
\le
\delta_{\rm true}(y)
\le
\delta_{\rm upper}(y).
}
\]

On \(N=129\),
\[
-0.0121013203533
\le
\delta(T_s)
\le
-0.0121013203460,
\]
while the full exact oracle gives
\[
\delta(T_s)
=
-0.0121013203478.
\]

On \(N=257\), the compressed bracket width was approximately
\[
1.6\times10^{-11}.
\]

Thus the compressed oracle reproduces the exact reduced Farkas geometry
to far better precision than required by the working safety scale
\[
\rho_0\sim 5\times10^{-4}.
\]

\subsection*{5. Failure of one-step contraction}

The hypothesis
\[
d_{2m}^{\rm safe}
\le
\lambda d_m^{\rm safe},
\qquad
\lambda<1,
\]
is numerically false.

For one orbit,
\[
0.012713
\longrightarrow
0.003798
\longrightarrow
0.010947,
\]
so that
\[
\frac{d_{513}}{d_{257}}
\approx
2.882.
\]

For an independently selected orbit,
\[
0.012713
\longrightarrow
0.006144
\longrightarrow
0.007228,
\]
so that
\[
\frac{d_{513}}{d_{257}}
\approx
1.176.
\]

Hence
\[
\boxed{
\text{one-step safe-deficit contraction is not the correct invariant.}
}
\]

The candidate invariant-tube principle nevertheless survives, since
explicit admissible descendants remain inside a reduced tube of width
approximately
\[
\epsilon_0\sim 10^{-2}
\]
with positive reduced margin.

\subsection*{6. Greedy current optimization is dynamically misaligned}

A controlled line search was performed on a single fixed \(N=129\)
parent state.

Two admissible parent couplings were chosen with reduced interior margins
approximately
\[
\rho_A=5\times10^{-4},
\qquad
\rho_B=10^{-3},
\]
and
\[
\pi_\theta
=
(1-\theta)\pi_A+\theta\pi_B.
\]

The parent target deficit increased monotonically:

\begin{array}{c|ccccc}

&

&

&

&

&

&

&

&

whereas the descendant safe deficit decreased:
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}

&

&

&

&

&
1
\\
\hline

&

&

The linear fit satisfies approximately

Thus on this particular state
\[
\frac{-\Delta d_{\rm child}}

\approx

An independent parent reproduced the sign of the effect but with much
smaller magnitude and an early turning point.

Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\text{the control signal exists, but its strength is state-dependent.}
}
\]

In particular,
\[
\boxed{
\text{minimizing current target distance is not a valid dynamic policy.}
}
\]

\subsection*{7. Parent reduced geometry does not explain the effect}

Two simple parent-level explanations were tested.

First, the number and degeneracy of active Farkas facets did not distinguish
the strongly responsive and weakly responsive parent states.

Second, the alignment of the parent interpolation direction
\[
\Delta y=y_B-y_A
\]
with the active Farkas normal was nearly identical:
\[
\cos\angle(\Delta y,z)
\approx
-0.235
\]
on one state and
\[
\cos\angle(\Delta y,z)
\approx
-0.217
\]
on the other.

Hence the large difference in descendant response is not explained by
the local three-dimensional parent geometry alone.

The first map is genuinely state-dependent, and the child Farkas geometry

\subsection*{9. Reduced profile is not a sufficient dynamic state}

A decisive numerical issue appeared when reconstructing a coupling from a
fixed reduced profile.

Two couplings can satisfy essentially the same reduced parent constraints,
while producing noticeably different descendants:
\[
F_m\pi_1
\approx
F_m\pi_2,
\]
yet
\[
\Child_m(\pi_1)
\not\approx
\Child_m(\pi_2).
\]

Consequently,
\[
\boxed{
y=(m,\mu_2,\mu_4)
\text{ is not by itself a Markov state for the renormalization dynamics.}
}
\]

The hidden fiber variable, referred to as the phase, affects future
viability.

A Bellman state based only on
\[
V_m(y)
\]
is therefore insufficient in general.

One needs either
\[
V_m(y,\Psi)
\]
or a lower-dimensional phase statistic
\[
V_m(y,\xi).
\]

\subsection*{10. Replicability intervention}

The previous conclusion appears to overstate the need to resolve the
phase with arbitrarily high precision.

The relevant lesson from the deterministic continuous DREAM-style
architecture is different:

\[
\boxed{
\text{replace exact optimization by a reproducible coarse decision.}
}
\]

The key structural pattern is
\[
\text{continuous dynamics}
\longrightarrow
\text{single deterministic coarse projection}
\longrightarrow
\text{exact verification}.
\]

For the WPB problem, let
\[
\xi_m(\pi)
\]
denote a finite-dimensional phase/control descriptor and introduce a
dyadic quantizer
\[
\boxed{
Q_b(x)
=
2^{-b}\operatorname{round}(2^b x).
}
\]

Rather than selecting an arbitrarily precise optimizer \(\pi^\ast\), define
a canonical anchor by
\[
\boxed{
\widehat\pi_m
=
C_m\!\left(Q_b(\xi_m(\pi))\right),
}
\]
where \(C_m\) is a fixed deterministic reconstruction rule.

The dynamic object is therefore not an exact phase point but a
\emph{replicable phase cell}
\[
\mathcal C_m(q)
=
\left\{
\pi:
Q_b(\xi_m(\pi))=q
\right\}.
\]

\subsection*{11. Accuracy--replicability trade-off}

Assume the descendant slack is locally Lipschitz with respect to the
chosen phase coordinate:
\[
\left|
\rho_{2m}(\pi)
-
\rho_{2m}(\widetilde\pi)
\right|
\le
L
\|
\xi_m(\pi)-\xi_m(\widetilde\pi)
\|.
\]

For coordinatewise dyadic quantization,
\[
\|
\xi-Q_b(\xi)
\|
\le
c\,2^{-b}.
\]

Hence
\[
\rho_{2m}(\widehat\pi)
\ge
\rho_{2m}(\pi)
-
Lc\,2^{-b}.
\]

Therefore a continuous anchor satisfying
\[
\rho_{2m}(\pi)
\ge
\rho_0
+
Lc\,2^{-b}
\]
produces a quantized canonical anchor satisfying
\[
\boxed{
\rho_{2m}(\widehat\pi)
\ge
\rho_0.
}
\]

Thus finite precision is not merely a numerical defect.
It can be used deliberately as a robustness mechanism:
\[
\boxed{
\text{precision is sacrificed in exchange for reproducibility.}
}
\]

\subsection*{12. Quantization margin}

To ensure invariance of the discrete phase label under small numerical
perturbations, define
\[
\operatorname{qmargin}_b(\xi)
=
\operatorname{dist}
\left(
\xi,
2^{-b}\left(\mathbb Z+\frac12\right)^d
\right),
\]
or coordinatewise
\[
\operatorname{qmargin}_b(\xi)
=
\min_j
\operatorname{dist}
\left(
\xi_j,
2^{-b}\left(\mathbb Z+\frac12\right)
\right).
\]

A robust canonical phase state should satisfy
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{qmargin}_b(\xi)
\ge
\gamma 2^{-b}
}
\]
for some scale-independent
\[
\gamma>0.
\]

Then sufficiently small floating-point, solver, or reconstruction
perturbations cannot change the quantized phase label.

\subsection*{13. Revised missing lemma}

The numerically motivated target is no longer an exact invariant phase
trajectory.

A more natural sufficient statement is the following.

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{Replicable Safe-Anchor Lemma.}
There exist constants
\[
b<\infty,
\qquad
\epsilon_0>0,
\qquad
\rho_0>0,
\qquad
\gamma>0,
\qquad
C_{\rm bill}<\infty,
\]
independent of the dyadic scale \(m\), together with a finite-dimensional
phase descriptor \(\xi_m\), a dyadic quantizer \(Q_b\), and a deterministic
canonical reconstruction \(C_m\), such that every admissible state in the
invariant tube has a prefix-compatible lift \(\pi_m\) satisfying
\[
\|F_m\pi_m-T_s\|_\infty
\le
\epsilon_0,
\]
\[
\delta_m(F_m\pi_m)
\ge
\rho_0,
\]
\[
\operatorname{qmargin}_b(\xi_m(\pi_m))
\ge
\gamma 2^{-b},
\]
and whose canonical quantized descendant again lies in the same tube:
\[
\boxed{
C_{2m}
\left(
Q_b\bigl(
\xi_{2m}(\Child_m(\pi_m))
\bigr)
\right)
\in
\mathfrak K_{2m}.
}
\]

In addition, the associated zero-repair inventory increment satisfies
\[
\boxed{
\Delta\mathcal B_m
\le
C_{\rm bill}.
}
\]

\subsection*{14. Consequence}

If the Replicable Safe-Anchor Lemma and the inventory-accounting bridge
are proved, then
\[
\mathcal B_{2n}
\le
\mathcal B_n+C_{\rm bill},
\]
and therefore
\[
\mathcal B_n
=
O(\log n).
\]

Combined with the already established lower bound,
\[
\mathcal B_n
=
\Omega(\log n),
\]

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal B_n
=

}
\]

\subsection*{15. Current interpretation}

The latest numerical evidence therefore suggests the following hierarchy:
\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c}
\text{exact reduced fixed point}
\\[1mm]
\Downarrow\ \text{too rigid}
\\[1mm]
\text{continuous invariant tube}
\\[1mm]
\Downarrow\ \text{still phase-sensitive}
\\[1mm]
\text{exact phase tracking}
\\[1mm]
\Downarrow\ \text{numerically non-replicable}
\\[1mm]
\textbf{finite-resolution replicable phase dynamics}.
\end{array}
}
\]

The central question has consequently changed from
\[
\text{``Which exact phase point is dynamically optimal?''}
\]
to
\[
\boxed{
\text{``How many robust phase bits per dyadic scale are sufficient
for safe continuation?''}
}
\]

This is presently the most concrete formulation of the remaining
upper-bound problem.
